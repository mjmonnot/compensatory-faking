# Study 2 — Empirical Anchor Table for the Trait-Driven Faking Simulation

Purpose: document empirically observed correlations/effect sizes to parameterize a Monte Carlo selection simulation in which applicant faking is driven by traits (not random). Every numeric estimate below was verified against fetched source text or computed from the local Stanek–Ones database (`db.pkl`); rows that could not be verified have blank estimates and are marked **needs full-text check**. Compiled 2026-08-11.

Design-type codes: **field** = genuine applicants, within-person high-/low-stakes; **lab** = simulated high-stakes or instructed faking; **interview** = interview-faking studies (self-reported faking behavior or intentions, mostly student/worker samples); **meta** = meta-analysis; **computed** = computed from the Stanek–Ones (2023) database for this project.

### 1f. Where findings CONFLICT across design types (deliberate feature of this table)

1. **Cognitive ability — sign flips by design.** Field within-person (Holtrop): r = **−.19** (higher-g applicants elevate *less*). Incentivized lab (Dunlop): positive predictor once gender controlled (partial r = **+.14**; significant positive in regressions). Lab interview simulation (Buehl & Melchers S2): faking-effectiveness r = **+.19**. Meta-analytic moderation (Schilling): g–personality correlations in selection contexts are ~2–3× larger in lab (ρ up to .35) than field (ρ up to .21). Interpretation: in the field, g may index a lower *need* to fake; in the lab, g indexes the *ability* to fake when motivation is held high.
2. **Honesty-humility — null in field, positive in one incentivized lab study, robustly negative in interview self-report.** Field (Holtrop): r = −.04 ns. Incentivized lab (Dunlop): r = **+.21** (driven by modesty; unexpected sign). Interview studies: consistently **−.15 to −.45**. A field-anchored personality-test simulation should use ≈0; a deceptive-IM/interview-flavored simulation should use a clearly negative slope.
3. **Magnitude of faking** scales with paradigm: field within-person d ≈ 0.1–0.2 on job-relevant traits (Holtrop) < applicant/non-applicant d ≈ 0.11–0.45 (Birkeland) ≈ incentivized-lab d_z 0.32–0.50 on targeted facets (Dunlop) < instructed faking d up to ~1.1 (MacCann).
4. **Faking norms/instrumentality:** null on direct faking in both field and lab (−.09, −.03), but positive on overclaiming in the field (+.19) and instrumentality positive in the lab (+.16) — motivational effects appear operationalization-dependent.

## Recommended parameter ranges

Model parameters: latent faking-propensity slopes (standardized) on honesty-humility/integrity (H), Machiavellianism (Mach), cognitive ability (g), true conscientiousness (C_true), and motivational inputs; plus inflation-magnitude components (mean shift d and faker mixture proportion).

### Field-anchored parameterization (personality-test faking by genuine applicants)

| Parameter | Range | One-line justification |
|---|---|---|
| Propensity slope on H/integrity | **−.10 to 0** | H–faking r = −.04 (ns) in the only direct field test (Holtrop); allow up to −.15 if faking construct includes deceptive IM (interview literature −.2 to −.4). |
| Propensity slope on Mach | **+.15 to +.35** | No field personality-test estimate exists; borrowed from interview faking *behavior* (.17–.43, Levashina & Campion; Roulin & Bourdage) — flag as the weakest field anchor. |
| Propensity slope on g | **−.20 to −.10** | Two independent field/recall estimates converge at r = −.19 (Holtrop; Buehl & Melchers S1). |
| Propensity slope on C_true | **−.10 to +.15** | Field estimate +.17 ns (Holtrop) vs. interview ≈ −.30; center near 0 for questionnaire faking. |
| Motivational slope (valence/desirability) | **0 to +.20** | No direct field measure; field norms/instrumentality proxies null on faking (−.09) but +.19 on overclaiming. |
| Inflation magnitude (mean d, job-relevant traits) | **0.10–0.25** | Holtrop d = 0.13–0.19; Hu & Connelly "moderately higher" means; Birkeland group d .11–.45 as ceiling. |
| Faker mixture proportion | **0.30–0.50** | Griffith et al. (2007): 30–50% of applicants elevated scores. |

### Lab-anchored parameterization (simulated high-stakes / maximal-motivation regime)

| Parameter | Range | One-line justification |
|---|---|---|
| Propensity slope on H/integrity | **−.20 to +.20** (sign uncertain; +.21 in the one incentivized test) | Dunlop found H *positively* related to faking (+.21, modesty-driven); classical instructed-faking studies (McFarland & Ryan) report integrity related to faking, but sign/magnitude unverifiable (full text not retrievable via open web as of 2026-08-12). |
| Propensity slope on Mach | **+.30 to +.45** | Faking intentions r = .45 (Roulin & Krings, N = 508); upper end of interview behavior estimates. |
| Propensity slope on g | **+.10 to +.20** | Dunlop partial r = +.14 (positive in all regressions); Buehl & Melchers S2 r = +.19; consistent with Schilling lab ρ up to .35. |
| Propensity slope on C_true | **−.30 to 0** | Interview C correlations ≈ −.30 (Roulin & Krings facets −.24 to −.36); McFarland & Ryan report C related to faking (sign/magnitude unverifiable; full text not retrievable via open web as of 2026-08-12). |
| Motivational slopes | valence **+.35 to +.40**; instrumentality/expectancy **+.15** each | Dunlop: r = .37 (valence), .16 (instrumentality), .15 (expectancy); valence dominates the combined model. |
| Inflation magnitude (mean d, targeted traits) | **0.30–0.50** (up to 1.1 for instructed-faking bound) | Dunlop d_z = 0.32–0.50 on targeted facets; MacCann instructed d = 0.49–1.08. |
| Faker mixture proportion | **≈ 0.27+** | Dunlop: 51/189 (27%) exceeded 1.96 × SEM elevation vs. ~5% expected by chance. |

### Parameters with NO usable empirical anchor (must be treated as sensitivity-analysis dimensions)

- **Mach → faking on personality tests in the field**: no within-person applicant study measures Machiavellianism; all estimates come from interview faking or intentions.
